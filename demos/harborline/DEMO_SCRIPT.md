## Script

{Opening, no screen yet}
Wisdom serves two personas. Business users, often non-technical decision makers, who need data and insight to do their jobs. And data administrators, who own governance and access and keep answers accurate and consistent. We will look at the product through both lenses, which gives the full picture of what is possible.

The scenario is fictional. Harborline Private Capital produces private-markets benchmarks and indices. It collects fund cash flows and NAVs from the LPs and GPs that contribute, extracts figures from fund quarterly reports, and publishes the Harborline Private Capital Index family. Its allocator clients, pensions and endowments, get the indices and today configure custom indices and pacing views by hand on a portal. Harborline wants a premium tier where a client just says what they need.

{New Live App, paste the prompt}
Let me be that client. "Build me a custom index of North American buyout funds, vintages 2015 to 2020, and compare quarterly IRR to the broad PE benchmark." That is the whole request. Wisdom is now writing the queries, the charts and the page. It takes a few minutes, so while that cooks let's look at the other side.

{Data-admin side: the standard domain-building walkthrough, your own flow}
The business user only gets a trustworthy answer because of what lives here. The domain sits on HARBORLINE_POC in Snowflake: funds, cash flows, quarterly NAVs, the published indices, allocator commitments, and a table of figures extracted from GP quarterly reports. Show the Calder Ridge Capital Partners IV Q2 2026 report attached as a document, the context extractor with the glossary, the index methodology standard and the index desk chat export already mined into knowledge, synonyms, certified metrics and reviewed queries, and the description rewrites left pending for a steward to review. Point at the Domain Health analysis running. This is the governance the data administrator owns.

{Back to the Live App: what it should have generated}
Page one is the index builder. Filters preset to Buyout, North America, vintages 2015 to 2020. The tiles should read about 49 funds, a median IRR of 15.9 percent against 13.3 percent for the Harborline Broad Private Equity Index, 2.6 points ahead, and a pooled TVPI of 1.58 against 1.54. The chart is the cohort's median IRR by quarter with the broad index as a second line; the table below breaks it out by vintage, 2020 the strongest at 18.6 percent pooled, 2016 the weakest at 9.2.
Now I refine it the way a client would. "Now split by fund size." Mid-market funds, one to five billion, lead at 16.3 percent; mega funds trail at 13.1; the published index is capital weighted, which is why the mega funds pull it down. "Add a cash-flow pacing view." The J-curve of calls and distributions by fund age, the cohort turning cash positive around year five, and next to it the 2021 and 2022 vintages with a DPI near 0.16 at four years where the cohort had about 0.56. That is the production version of translating plain English into a configured portal view. {If the live build drifts, switch to the pre-built Custom Index Studio and show the same three pages.}

{Ask a follow-up inside the Live App}
There is always a next question, and it is asked right here. "Compare Westbrook State Retirement System's commitments by vintage year to the matching Harborline benchmarks and tell me which vintage lags the most and which funds explain the gap." Wisdom runs a multi-step analysis: commitment-weighted IRR per vintage, each fund's matching index at its own vintage year, then the funds behind the gap. The headline: 2018 is the only vintage below benchmark, 8.5 percent against 12.0, about 3.5 points behind, and two venture funds explain it, Bramblewood Ventures III fifteen points under its index and Sable Point Ventures II twelve under. Every other vintage is at or above. The evidence and the SQL are one click away.
Then the extraction beat. "What does the Calder Ridge IV Q2 2026 report say about its subscription line and does it match the NAV we hold?" Wisdom reads the PDF, 400 million facility, 210 million drawn, 8 percent of NAV, NAV of 2,614 million, and confirms the extraction table and the published figure agree to the decimal, confidence 0.97. Reading reports is a core part of the index team's workflow; here it is part of the same governed answer.

{Agent tab}
If I want to automate a question, I do it with an agent. Subscription Line Watch runs after every quarterly refresh. It lists funds whose credit line is above 15 percent of NAV, five today, and flags any young vintage where funds with a line show an IRR more than three points above funds without, about five points at eight quarters for the 2019 to 2022 vintages. It investigates what moved, then sends Index Research the finding with the data attached. Like everything else here, it was generated from a single prompt in plain English.

## Agent prompt to pre-build
Create an agent called "Subscription Line Watch" on the Harborline Private Capital domain. On the first Monday after each quarterly data refresh (next run after 2026-12-29 at 7am), look at the latest quarter end in V_FUND_PERFORMANCE_LATEST. For every fund with USES_SUBSCRIPTION_LINE true, calculate CREDIT_LINE_PCT_OF_NAV and list the funds above 15 percent with their manager, strategy, vintage year, NAV, line balance and line limit. Then using V_SUBSCRIPTION_LINE_IRR_GAP, for vintages less than four years old at that quarter end, find every vintage and strategy where IRR_GAP_PTS (funds with a line minus funds without at the same fund age) is above 3 points and FUNDS_WITH_LINE and FUNDS_WITHOUT_LINE are both at least 3. If anything triggers, investigate which funds drive it, whether their line balance rose quarter on quarter, and whether their quarterly return changed, and send Index Research a summary with the finding, the affected funds and cohorts, and the supporting data. Never repeat the same alert in consecutive quarters for the same fund and send nothing if nothing triggers.
