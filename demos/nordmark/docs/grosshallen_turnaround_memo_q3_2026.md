# Grosshallen Turnaround — Q3 2026 Review Memo

Letterhead: Nordmark Dagligvaror AB — Office of the Banner Director, Grosshallen
To: Group CEO, Group CFO, Head of Store Operations
From: Maria Holm, Banner Director Grosshallen
Date: 2026-09-29
Classification: Internal, board preparation

## 1. Summary

Grosshallen has reached break-even. Q3 2026 store EBIT is about +SEK 9 million against about -SEK 35 million in Q3 2025, and the banner is at break-even on a rolling basis for the first time since the acquisition on 2025-01-01. The improvement comes from structural measures, not from sales: like-for-like sales are still about 1.4 percent below last year over the rolling 12 months while the group grows about 1.3 percent and Torghallen about 4.8 percent. The cost work is done; the next phase is customer traffic.

## 2. What drove the result

- Store labour fell from about 13.5 percent of sales in 2025 to about 11.3 percent in Q3 2026 through the new scheduling model.
- Inbound logistics moved into the group network in the spring, taking about 0.5 points of cost out.
- Grosshallen Sundsvik (Helsingborg), the smallest and weakest store, closed on 2026-08-15 and reopened as Kronmat Sundsvik on 2026-09-05. It is the fifth and last planned structural change to the estate; the 37 remaining stores are expected to stay.
- Gross margin is stable at about 24.5 percent, 2.3 points below Kronmat and 6 points below Torghallen; margin was never the Grosshallen problem.

## 3. What has not improved

Transactions. Over the rolling 12 months comparable Grosshallen stores lost about 5.6 percent of transactions while the average basket grew about 4.4 percent. Fewer households do their big weekly shop with us, and those who stay buy more per trip. The decomposition is the same in almost every store: the sales change is a transactions effect, not a basket effect.

Three factors recur in the store reviews:

1. Availability. Fresh availability is below the group in most Grosshallen stores, and Dairy and Bread & Bakery, the categories that anchor the weekly trip, are the weakest.
2. Discount competition. Where a discounter has opened within 2 km, Families visits fall first and furthest.
3. Private label. Grosshallen's private-label share (about 19 percent) is well below Kronmat (about 30 percent); price perception suffers although our price index is competitive.

## 4. Grosshallen Västerhamn

Västerhamn (Göteborg, Suburban family catchment, 5,420 sqm) is the store that matters most. Rolling-12-month sales are about SEK 161 million against a catchment potential of about SEK 259 million: a potential index of 0.62 against a cluster median of 0.92 and a gap of about SEK 99 million, the largest in the banner. Sales fell 7.5 percent year on year; transactions fell 11 percent.

- Billigmat opened a discount store 1.1 km away on 2026-03-12. Västerhamn's sales in the six months after the opening were about 9.5 percent below the same months a year earlier.
- Families visits are down about 23 percent; the other segments are down about 10 percent.
- Dairy and Bread & Bakery availability over the last 13 weeks is about 89 percent, the worst in the banner, against a 96 percent target.
- Markdown rate is about 2.0 percent of sales against 1.3 percent for the cluster; private-label share 14 percent against 17 percent.
- Non-food: availability of the imported range is about 91 percent, and lost sales concentrate in SKUs from three suppliers whose purchase orders arrive late in the purchasing platform (Sterling Trading, Frontier Trading, Lakeshore Industries).

## 5. The peer: Grosshallen Nordstaden

Nordstaden (Uppsala) sits in the same peer cluster with a similar catchment (about 23,000 residents within 2 km, a discounter nearby) and captures about 97 percent of its potential with like-for-like growth of about +3.5 percent. The differences are operational: availability about 98 percent, private-label share about 21 percent, a markdown rate about 40 percent below Västerhamn, and a weekly Families offer through Grosshallen Kort since autumn 2025.

## 6. Recommendation

Run the Nordstaden playbook as a 12-week test at Västerhamn starting the week of 2026-10-12:

- Availability: revised Relex order parameters for Dairy and Bread & Bakery, a second daily bread delivery, a store-level availability target of 96 percent.
- Assortment: relist the private-label core range to lift private-label share above 20 percent.
- Price and promotion: replace blanket markdowns with targeted Families offers through Grosshallen Kort; keep the price index unchanged.
- Non-food: move the three late suppliers' core SKUs to the group's safety-stock profile while purchasing renegotiates lead times.

Measure weekly against Nordstaden and the cluster: transactions, Families visits, OSA, private-label share, markdown rate, gross margin. Success is transactions back within 5 percent of the prior year by week 12. Closing half the gap to the cluster median is worth roughly SEK 40 million of annual sales; the test costs about SEK 1.5 million. If it works, the same playbook applies to the next four stores on the gap ranking.
